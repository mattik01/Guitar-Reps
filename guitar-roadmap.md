# Guitar Roadmap & How to Practice

Condensed from Simon's guitar roadmap (playwithaplan.com). 8 levels across 3 phases, plus a practice framework.

> **Core idea:** Guitar is like climbing steps. Skip a step and the next one is really hard; understand it and the next becomes easy.

---

## Beginner (Levels 1–3)

Overriding priority: **just play a lot.** Volume and reps matter more than what exactly you practice.

### Level 1 — Complete beginner
- Tuning, how to hold the guitar, and how to hold & use the pick
- Learn the **string names** and **single notes** (single notes before chords — easier)
- Read tabs and chord diagrams
- Basic minor & major open chords
- Basic strumming
- Simple one-riff songs you like

### Level 2
- First position of the **pentatonic scale**
- **12-bar blues** (rhythm + simple improvisation)
- More colorful open chords: **sus2, sus4**
- More strumming patterns
- Play songs matching your skill level

### Level 3
- Learn & **understand** the notes of the fretboard (not just memorize — see how it's connected)
- All **5 positions of the pentatonic scale**, in all keys
- Introduction to **bar chords** (genuinely hard — you don't suck)
- Deeper rhythm guitar: combine chords, groove, and fills
- Play songs matching your skill level

### Beginner mistakes
1. **Not being patient** — 90% of players quit within their first year. Don't quit and you're ahead.
2. **Not enough playing** — get in the reps.
3. **Looking for the perfect routine** — what you play barely matters; playing does. Play what you enjoy.

---

## Intermediate (Levels 4–6)

Where most players plateau and get stuck. Level 4 is a critical foundation — do it in order.

### Level 4
- Understand & play the shapes of the **major scale** (almost all music is built on it — understanding is essential)
- **Seventh chords**
- Technique fundamentals: right hand, left hand, alternate picking, legato, fingerpicking
- Improvisation & soloing fundamentals — go from scales to **musical phrases**
- Tasty phrasing: creative slides and bends

### Level 5
*(You're quite good now — from here, skills can be learned in a more flexible order.)*
- Major & minor bar chords across the whole neck (e.g. **CAGED system**)
- The **7 modes** of the major scale
- **Economy picking** (a game changer)
- Deeper legato; **seventh arpeggios** across the whole neck

### Level 6
- Seventh chords across the whole neck
- Extended chords: **6, 9, add9**
- The **altered scale** (over dominant chords)
- Two-note-per-string arpeggios (useful, not a must)
- Double stops

### Intermediate mistakes
1. **Not practicing the basics enough** — pros know the pentatonic scale *super* well. Keep getting better at basics.
2. **Not learning basic theory** — the two essentials: notes of the fretboard + theory of the major scale. That's an incredible foundation. (Skip theory only if you just want to play songs.)
3. **Not pushing technique** — get intentional; add technique exercises. Great technique lets you play anything.

---

## Advanced (Levels 7–8)

Go deep. Never really "finish" — always new ways to improve.

### Level 7
- Fancier chords
- Beautiful chord progressions with **non-diatonic harmony** (beyond the major scale)
- **Hybrid picking**
- Extended arpeggios (e.g. 9th arpeggios)
- Advanced applications of the pentatonic scale
- Soloing/improvising over chord changes

### Level 8
- **Fourth chords**, **drop-2 voicings**
- More extended chords: **11, 13**
- Advanced arpeggio use
- **Melodic minor** and other fancy scales
- Add whatever fits your goals: songwriting, ear training, etc.

### Advanced mistake
- **Stopping learning.** You never finish guitar — keep finding new ways to improve.

---

## How to practice: the L-E-C framework

For anything on the roadmap:

1. **L — Lesson:** Find a lesson that explains the concept (blog post, teacher, YouTube). Get the information.
2. **E — Exercise:** Find or create good exercises and actually practice it. Watching ≠ learning.
3. **C — Challenge:** Use the skill in real music (e.g. improvise over a backing track). This is the only thing that ultimately matters.
